Chess in Naples, Florida: The Complete Guide

Where to play chess in Naples, what a club night actually involves, what it costs, and how to start playing over the board if you are new to the area or new to the game.

  • Naples Chess Club meets every Wednesday from 6:30 PM to 8:00 PM at Narrative Coffee Roasters in Mercato, 9110 Strada Pl, Naples, FL 34108.
  • Some weeks we play elsewhere in Naples. The week's location always goes up on our Instagram, so check there before setting off.
  • It is free. There are no dues, no membership and nothing to sign up for.
  • All ages and all skill levels are welcome, including complete beginners and children with a parent.
  • You do not need to bring a set, a clock or any equipment.

Naples has one of the better climates in the country for playing chess outdoors, and a population with the time and inclination to do it. What it does not have is an obvious front door. If you have just moved here, or you have lived here for years and recently picked the game back up, it is not always clear where people actually meet.

This guide covers everything worth knowing: where games happen, what an evening looks like, what it costs, what to bring, and how to get started if you have not touched a board since school.

Where to play chess in Naples

The most reliable option is a club that meets on a fixed schedule, because you know people will be there. Casual play in public spaces is pleasant but unpredictable.

Naples Chess Club, Narrative Coffee Roasters

Our regular home is Narrative Coffee Roasters at Mercato, 9110 Strada Pl, every Wednesday evening from 6:30 PM to 8:00 PM. Parking at Mercato is free, including the garage. Look for the tables with boards on them.

Some weeks we play somewhere else in Naples. When that happens we post it on Instagram ahead of time, so a quick look before you leave is worth the ten seconds it takes.

There is no sign-up sheet and no committee. You walk up, someone offers you a game, and you play.

Parks and public tables

Southwest Florida's weather makes outdoor chess practical for most of the year in a way it is not in most of the country. Shaded tables in public spaces do attract casual games, particularly in the cooler months.

The trade-off is reliability. You might find an opponent, or you might find an empty table and drive home. That unpredictability is the specific problem a weekly club solves.

Libraries and community centres

Collier County libraries and community centres sometimes run chess sessions, often aimed at juniors and usually during the day. Schedules change term by term, so it is worth calling ahead rather than turning up hopefully.

Online play

Most of us play online too, and it is a genuinely good way to get volume. But it is a different activity. Online chess is fast, anonymous and endless. Over the board chess is slower, social, and far more memorable. People remember games they played across a table years later. They rarely remember a blitz game from Tuesday.

If you only play online, the jump to a physical board is worth making at least once.

What an evening actually looks like

People are usually more nervous about walking up to a chess club than about the chess itself, which is fair. Turning up somewhere new on your own is the awkward part.

Here is the whole process. You arrive any time after 6:30. Someone says hello and asks if you want a game. You sit down and play.

That is it. No introductions in front of the group, no roll call, no form.

On a typical evening you will find a mix running at once:

  • Casual games where both players chat the whole way through
  • Quieter, more serious games between stronger players
  • Someone teaching a beginner, often mid-game
  • At least one game where neither player is quite sure what is happening

Strength varies enormously. Some regulars have played in rated tournaments. Others learned last year. People are good about pairing up sensibly so nobody has a miserable time.

Nobody is keeping score of who you beat. The point is to play.

What it costs

Nothing. Chess night is free, there are no dues, no membership fee and nothing to pay at the door. This is a group of people who like chess, not a business.

If you are a beginner

Say so when you sit down. It genuinely changes the evening for the better.

Telling an opponent "I have not played since school" means they will slow down, explain what they are doing, and probably let you take moves back. Most chess players are delighted that another person wants to play at all, and teaching is part of the appeal for them.

If you want to arrive with a little preparation, you do not need to study openings. Five or six principles will take you further than any memorised line. We wrote those up as a one-page checklist you can print: see the chess opening principles checklist.

Bringing children

Juniors are welcome, and we are family friendly. Plenty of parents play their own game at the next table while their child plays theirs.

Children improve remarkably fast when they play adults who are patient with them, and a club is one of the few places that happens naturally. It is also a better shared activity than drop-off-and-collect lessons, because you are both doing the same thing.

The season in Naples

Naples has a rhythm that affects everything, chess included. Numbers climb from roughly November through April as seasonal residents return, then settle over the summer.

Both halves of the year have their appeal. Busier evenings mean more opponents and more variety. Quieter summer sessions tend to produce longer, more thoughtful games, and more teaching.

The night itself does not change. Wednesday, 6:30 PM.

Check before you come

Narrative Coffee Roasters is where you will find us most weeks, but not every week. We sometimes move to another spot around Naples depending on what is on.

The week's location always goes up on our Instagram. Checking it takes a few seconds and it beats driving over to the wrong place. If you would rather just ask, send us a text and we will tell you where we are.

Does chess actually do anything for you?

You will have seen the claim that chess makes you smarter, improves children's maths scores, or protects against dementia. Some of that is better supported than the rest, and the honest answer is more interesting than the marketing version.

We went through the actual studies in does chess make you smarter, including the large prospective study that found board games associated with lower dementia risk in older adults, and the meta-analyses that are far more skeptical about chess improving school performance.

Frequently asked questions

Where does Naples Chess Club meet?

Narrative Coffee Roasters at Mercato, 9110 Strada Pl, Naples, FL 34108. We meet every Wednesday from 6:30 PM to 8:00 PM, and parking at Mercato is free. Some weeks we play elsewhere in Naples, so check our Instagram before you set off.

How much does it cost to join?

Nothing. There are no dues, no membership fee and nothing to sign up for. Chess night is free to attend.

Do I need to be good at chess?

No. Complete beginners and experienced tournament players sit at the same tables every week. If you know how the pieces move, you know enough to turn up.

Do I need to bring a chess set?

No. We bring sets and boards. If you own a set you like, you are welcome to bring it, because more boards means more games running at the same time.

Can I bring my children?

Yes. All ages are welcome and we are family friendly. Many parents play their own game while their children play theirs.

Do I have to stay for the whole session?

No. Some people play one game and leave, others stay from 6:30 through to 8:00. Coming and going as it suits you is completely normal.

Does the location ever change?

Yes, occasionally. Narrative Coffee Roasters at Mercato is our regular home, but some weeks we play somewhere else around Naples. We always post the week's location on Instagram, so check there before heading over, or send us a text and we will tell you.

Is there a club for juniors specifically?

Our Wednesday evenings are open to all ages rather than split by age group. Collier County libraries and some schools run junior-specific sessions during term time, which are worth looking into if you want something aimed only at children.
